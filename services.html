HOW EACH SERVICE WORKS

Manned guarding, mobile patrols and alarm response, in detail.

Manned Guarding

Our officers match the profile your site needs — a friendly, welcoming presence for visitors, or a firm, no-nonsense approach protecting equipment and machinery overnight. Every officer is screened, vetted and trained to anticipate a problem, diffuse tension with tact, and respond to difficult situations calmly and quickly.

Mobile Patrols

Crime prevention is the priority, but we respond quickly when it isn't enough. Assigned to your premises and surroundings, our patrols check even the most remote corners of a site — on foot, by vehicle, or with dogs — with randomised routes that keep unwelcome visitors guessing. Where a dedicated on-site team isn't practical, mobile patrols offer an affordable alternative.

Alarm Response

Linked to our key-holding service, alarm response protects your premises around the clock. As soon as the alarm receiving centre gets a signal, our uniformed response officers and liveried vehicles are on their way — often reaching site before the police. We check for intruders, damage and forced entry, make the premises safe, call in preferred locksmiths or glaziers, and liaise directly with emergency services.

24/7 Control Room

Our officers are closely supervised by team leaders and managers with genuine local knowledge, backed by a round-the-clock control room. This state-of-the-art nerve centre supports static officers and mobile patrols in the field, monitors lone workers in isolated or demanding workplaces, and is always a phone call away from our senior managers.

OUR APPROACH

A five-phase site security survey.

Before we deploy a single officer, we assess the property, the street and the neighbourhood — identifying risk, recommending reductions, and understanding the balance between security and safety.

  1. The core

    Assessing the inside of the premises itself — what needs protecting, and from what.

  2. The outer security area

    The yard, store or parking area surrounding the core of the site.

  3. The perimeter

    The security fence or boundary — your first line of protection.

  4. The surroundings

    The wider neighbourhood, and how it affects risk to your site.

  5. Access & egress control

    Procedures governing who comes in, who goes out, and how that's verified.
